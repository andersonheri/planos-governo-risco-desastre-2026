Six takeaways

1
Almost all talk, few promise

{{P_MENCAO}}% mention the topic, but only {{P_ACAO}}% have concrete action in most passages. Only {{PE4}}% of passages carry a target, deadline, indicator or budget.

2
Only {{N_PROM}} candidates put money behind the promise

Tarcísio (SP), R$ 25 billion by 2029; Carlos Machado (SP), R$ 1.5 billion; Ben Mendes (MG), R$ 300 to 520 million. The rest is past spending, criticism or another topic.

3
Plans look at what comes before the disaster

Prevention and preparedness in {{P_PREV}}% of candidates; response in {{P_RESP}}%; recovery in {{P_REC}}%. Only {{N_CICLO}} cover the whole cycle.

4
Plans do not follow the state's disaster

In Piauí, drought is {{PI_SHARE}}% of records, yet only {{PI_CITA}}% of candidates cite it. {{N_NC}} do not cite their own state's main disaster.

5
Those who suffer most do not promise more

The intensity of disasters in each state is unrelated to how concrete its plans are (Spearman correlation of {{RHO}}).

6
Plan length matters more than ideology

The topic appears in {{P_ME_5MIL}}% of plans under 5,000 words and in {{P_ME_20MIL}}% of those over 20,000. Concreteness does not differ across political camps.
